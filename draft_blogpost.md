'The Jesus Website' aims to catalogue about 300 data points (people, locations, events, parables etc) relating to Jesus and his context. Wikipedia - humanity's current open-source encyclopedia has articles about nearly all of these topics. However because they're open-sourced they are both uneven (some articles have had more constructive attention then others), and reflect some of the difficulties associated with the history of Jesus. The purpose of the algorithm is to use a matrix of 25 weights to rank the pool of 255 Wikipedia articles for reliability. Some of the weights are based on simple searches or curated lists, while other weights are based on small vector-embeding databases.  

I had an AI agent gather a pool of articles about Jesus, excluding at the offset anything didn't match the focus of my website. This pool then became the ranked list of 255 articles. The Table below describes each weight and provides a summary of how I processed its weight. (While the vector database is stored on the server, you can view the scripts and skills on Github.) Each article has a theoretical postive ceiling of about about 80 points. The base of the score is a grounding in the primary sources, plus an emphasis on an anylatical structure that seperates data or information from context and interpretation, and values debate within the interpretation / context section. Given the focus on Jesus; New Testament manuscript, ante-Nicean authors and Jesus' Jewish context are all given positive weighting. Some psoitive weights are category specific eg IAA sites for location or literary anayslis for parables. 

Mythcist, Gnostic, critical and anti-Supernatural presuppositions (I use as bias as the colloplquial term) outside the interpretation section are given negative weights. This is because the majority or dominate view of Jesus is a secular cirtical one, therefore those presupposition needs extra examination. Both for the sake of logical consistency, a critical view needs criticism, but also to put the breaks on overusing the sociological tool of 'consensus'. Consensus is a valuble, but insufficient tool on its own for determining truth.
