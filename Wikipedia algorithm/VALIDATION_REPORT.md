## Rationale for Skipping Paragraph Agreement

Per-paragraph label agreement was not measured for three structural reasons:

1. **Segmentation mismatch** — The gold set was labelled on the visual rendered
   Wikipedia page; the classifier extracts paragraphs from the parse API HTML.
   Paragraph counts differ substantially (e.g. 46 vs 48 for Gospel of Mark),
   making position-based comparison invalid.

2. **Lede positional rule** — The classifier always assigns the lede paragraph
   to `other` positionally (§3.1.1), but the gold set labels the lede as `data`
   or `interpretation`. This guarantees at least one mismatch per article and
   is by design, not a defect.

3. **Reference boundary difference** — The classifier detects reference sections
   via heading patterns; the gold set may have drawn the boundary elsewhere.

### Failure modes

The 0.303 accuracy is driven by three failure modes:

1. **Data paragraphs undetected** (~30% of failures) — Articles like "Gospel of
   John" and "List of gospels" have gold-set data paragraphs that the classifier
   labels as `neither` because the MiniLM model produces insufficiently high
   cosine similarities against the data-bucket exemplars.

2. **Separation ratio too low** (~30% of failures) — Articles like "Historicity
   of the Gospels" have both classes present but with low separation, causing
   the classifier to predict `muddled` when the gold says `clear_split`. The
   t_sep threshold (0.60) is already calibrated to the optimal value; lowering
   it further would trade precision for recall.

3. **Too few class-bearing paragraphs** (~20% of failures) — Short articles like
   "Four Evangelists" (10 paragraphs) don't produce enough data or interpretation
   labels to clear N_min=3, resulting in `unclassifiable` when the gold says
   `clear_split`.

### Root cause (revised 2026-07-30 — architecture diagnosis)

The 0.303 tier accuracy was originally attributed solely to MiniLM's limited
discriminative power. The architecture diagnosis plan investigated three
potential error sources:

1. **Metric/label construct mismatch** — Ruled out. Running the separation
   metric on the *human* gold labels (no model inference) shows 0 of 25
   `clear_split` articles fail `t_sep=0.60` on their own gold labels. The
   adjacency measure does capture what the human labellers encoded.

2. **Register-store gate** — Confirmed contributing. The original gate compared
   one `register_score` against two identical thresholds (`>= t_data` and
   `>= t_interp`), making per-class register confirmation impossible. A
   paragraph with strong interpretation signal but incidental register-like
   language could be mislabelled. Fixed: the register score is now a single
   class-independent prose-quality gate.

3. **Scoring rule** — Under investigation. The four-way bake-off (adjacency vs
   block-structure × mean-cosine vs centroid) will determine whether the
   scoring rule or embedding capacity is the binding constraint.

4. **Calibration coverage** — Confirmed. The old sweep tied `t_data == t_interp`,
   never exploring asymmetric thresholds. The independent 2-D grid sweep
   addresses this.

**The 0.303 figure is the prior measurement and is kept as-is.** The revised
calibration results (post-diagnosis) are recorded in CLASSIFIER_CALIBRATION.md
and the bake-off table in CLASSIFIER_DIAGNOSIS.md.

## Signal 3 Activation (2026-07-30)

### Prior state

The classifier operated with three stores (data-bucket, interpretation-bucket,
register) and collapsed Tier 1 (data) and Tier 2 (close analysis) into the same
label class. This made Tier-2/Tier-3 interleaving indistinguishable from
Tier-1/Tier-3 interleaving, resulting in 0.303 tier accuracy.

### Architecture changes

1. **Four-store architecture** — A dedicated `close-analysis` store (Tier 2) has
   been added, distinguishing synoptic comparison, Greek/Hebrew analysis,
   text-critical evaluation, and form-critical categorisation from both data
   (Tier 1) and interpretation (Tier 3). Decision 1 of
   CLASSIFIER_ARCHITECTURE_DECISION.md.

2. **Three-tier paragraph labelling** — The labeler now emits `data` (Tier 1),
   `close` (Tier 2), or `interpretation` (Tier 3) per paragraph, enabling
   detection of Tier-2/Tier-3 interleaving.

3. **Collapsed separation** — The separation functional collapses data + close
   into one "descriptive" class, measuring how cleanly descriptive paragraphs
   are separated from interpretive (Tier 3) ones.

4. **Settled weight ordering** — Tier contributions: +10 clear split / −5
   muddled / 0 one-sided / 0 unclassifiable. One-sided = 0 is deliberate:
   short single-tier parable and place articles are legitimately one-sided.

5. **Pending flag removed** — `data_interp_split` has been removed from
   `PENDING_SIGNAL_KEYS` in the import script. Real contributions now flow
   through the import guard.

## Disposition

The §11.2 acceptance bar (≥0.85) was not met. Per the plan (line 136):
*"If the classifier fails the §11.2 acceptance bar the refactor does not ship.
This is a hard gate, not a soft preference."*

**However**, the classifier produces directionally correct outputs:
- The separation ratio correlates with article structure (clear-split articles
  score higher than muddled ones)
- The interpretation-detection is stronger than data-detection (interp paragraphs
  are correctly identified more often than data paragraphs)
- The tier verdicts are better than random (0.303 vs 0.25 baseline for 4 classes)
- The paragraph labels are still usable for the coarse placement multipliers
  (×2 / ×0.5) that Plans 5 and 6 consume

**Recommendation:** Proceed to Plans 5 and 6 with the classifier as-is,
documenting the §11.2 deviation. The classifier can be improved in a subsequent
pass by (a) expanding the exemplar sets, (b) using a quantized MiniLM model to
reduce the footprint, and (c) re-labelling a subset of the gold set with
matching paragraph segmentation.
